## Human-First Contract

- People should not need to understand branches, tokens, scopes, or deployment systems to participate.
- Safe actions may be automated.
- Actions affecting accounts, files, privacy, reputation, identity, or money require plain-language explanation and approval.
- Every consequential action should support preview, audit, and correction.
- Technical errors should be translated into understandable outcomes without hiding the underlying record.

## Identity and representation

Crown Index should distinguish:

- verified person;
- self-declared identity;
- creator identity;
- fictional character;
- organization;
- Star Coin;
- Avatar Coin;
- anonymous or private participant.

Typing a public figure's name never creates verification, consent, representation, or endorsement.

## Proposal lifecycle

```text
Idea
→ Draft
→ Evidence collection
→ Public explanation
→ Review
→ Revision
→ Vote or authorized decision
→ Implementation
→ Audit
→ Appeal or correction
```

Every proposal should show:

- author;
- affected systems;
- rights and costs;
- evidence and counterevidence;
- conflicts of interest;
- implementation owner;
- review date;
- amendment history.

## Treasury rules

Treasury records must separate:

- internal points;
- utility tokens;
- collectible coins;
- coupons and access rights;
- asking prices;
- estimates;
- speculative values;
- completed sales;
- public funds;
- private wallet balances.

Attention, likes, internal rewards, or a typed price are not completed-market evidence.

## Research governance

```text
Level 0 — Idea
Level 1 — Personal observation
Level 2 — Instrument reading
Level 3 — Repeatable local result
Level 4 — Independent replication
Level 5 — Validated model
```

Governance decisions using scientific or technical claims should display the evidence level and unresolved uncertainty.

## AI governance

AI agents may:

- read approved sources;
- summarize;
- compare evidence;
- draft proposals;
- detect contradictions;
- prepare implementation previews;
- maintain public logs.

AI agents may not independently:

- impersonate a citizen or official;
- cast a binding human vote;
- spend public or private funds;
- suppress lawful criticism;
- delete governance history;
- secretly alter ranking or outcomes;
- publish a consequential decision without authorization.
